# SOC Central System Business Principles

## Purpose

Define the permanent business principles that guide architecture, automation, customer experience, and future development decisions.

## Core Principles

### 1. AI Is Not the Product

AI is not the product. A simpler business is the product.

Technology must reduce complexity, repetitive work, travel, and unnecessary customer effort.

### 2. Choose the Easiest Customer Path

Always prefer the path requiring the least time, effort, technical knowledge, and commitment from the prospect or customer.

The primary onboarding path should use the iPhone because customers can act immediately without returning to a computer.

### 3. Create Success Before Selling

Allow prospects to experience the value of SOC by creating and sending their first free card before asking them to buy.

The main call to action is:

**Create Your First FREE Card**

### 4. Move Personal Contact Later

Do not require an appointment before the prospect understands or experiences the product.

Use personal time after the prospect has shown interest, created a card, asked questions, or is ready to sign up.

### 5. Preserve Valuable Human Relationships

Use AI to handle repetitive prospecting, explanation, reminders, training, and administration.

Use June’s personal attention where trust, strategy, problem-solving, and sales conversations add genuine value.

### 6. Mobile First, Computer Optional

The 3-minute iPhone demonstration is the primary card-creation path.

The 7-minute computer tutorial is optional and is offered only to people who prefer creating cards on a computer.

### 7. Guide Actions, Not Just Content

The system should not merely show videos or send information.

Every message, video, page, and follow-up should guide the customer toward one clear next action.

### 8. One Connected Customer Journey

Prospecting, First Impression Email, free-card creation, appointment, sign-up, onboarding, and relationship marketing must operate as one connected journey.

Customer information must not be duplicated across separate systems.

### 9. Automate Carefully

Automate approved and repeatable work.

Retain human review for sensitive communication, uncertain information, exceptions, and important customer-facing decisions.

### 10. Keep the System Simple

Build one engine at a time.

Do not add features, integrations, documents, or complexity unless they clearly support the business goal or simplify the customer experience.

### SOC First, Future Possibilities Later

Design and complete the simplest reliable system for the SOC business first.

Remember possible future applications, but do not add multi-industry complexity to the current SOC system.

### Partner Ready Without Becoming Complicated

Separate partner settings from shared SOC logic so the same approved system can be configured for other SOC partners without redesigning the engines.

## Design Review Test

Before approving a new feature, ask:

1. Does this make the business simpler?
2. Does this reduce effort for the customer?
3. Does this help the customer take a clear next action?
4. Does this preserve personal attention for valuable conversations?
5. Does this fit the approved customer journey?
6. Is this the simplest reliable solution?

If the answer is no, revise or reject the feature.
